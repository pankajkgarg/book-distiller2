# Kimi K3 prompt optimization — feedback checkpoint

Date: 2026-07-18

The loop is intentionally paused for user feedback before testing a second nonfiction book or changing the prompt again. All reader-facing prose in these candidates was generated by Kimi K3. GPT-5.6 was used only for source inspection, chapter mapping, experiment design, and evaluation.

## User decision and observations

- The user explicitly selected A: “Loved option A.” It is the editorial champion.
- B is substantially more natural and cheaper, but it mirrors much of the source structure and retains nearly three quarters of the chapter.
- C demonstrates that stronger qualitative deletion language can backfire: it retained even more source and reduced excerpt share.
- D obeyed several explicit omit directives and had the lowest token use, but still expanded most retained items close to source depth.
- E shows that low reasoning is not reliably cheaper for this prompt: it exhausted a 24,000-token generation envelope in deliberation and returned zero visible prose. Its automatically proposed 36,000-token retry was stopped immediately. Starvation retries are now opt-in.

The next authorized experiment keeps A's writing contract, relaxes exact word-count arithmetic into a chapter-specific editorial band, and tests one held-out pivotal chapter from *Atomic Habits* at high reasoning.

## Held-out test: *Atomic Habits*, Chapter 1

The upstream map classified Chapter 1 as pivotal. Kimi received a flexible 40–60% editorial band centered at 50%, plus an 85% readability backstop. The prompt explicitly said not to count words precisely.

| Run | Effort | Source words | Expected band | Output words | Source retained | Excerpts | API output tokens | Seconds |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `v3v_flexible_atomic_habits_pivotal_high` | high | 4,388 | 1,755–2,633 | 3,296 | 75.1% | 490 words (14.9%) | 14,517 | 481 |

Mechanical coverage is complete: the British Cycling evidence, compounding argument, delayed-results/ice-cube model, Plateau of Latent Potential, all four systems-versus-goals problems, and the definition of an atomic habit are present. The completion boundary is valid.

Editorially, the run is substantially too source-like. It exceeded the 60% band and ignored several upstream cuts: it retained the long catalogue of compounding domains, multiple threshold analogies after the ice cube had established the idea, multiple systems examples, and near-source-length treatments of all four goal problems. This is useful negative evidence: a broad readability escape hatch plus “represent every section” lets K3 justify preserving almost everything.

Quote evaluation found 10 blockquote groups: 6 strict contiguous matches and 8 content matches after removing decorative outer quotation marks. At least one remaining mismatch is a straight-versus-curly apostrophe change; another is attributable to extraction whitespace around emphasized text. The result therefore does not clear the prompt's exact-punctuation quotation contract without a deterministic excerpt-validation/repair pass.

The run does not replace A. Further Kimi iteration is paused for user prose feedback.
